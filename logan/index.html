Common Water Damage Issues in Logan

In Logan, the same handful of problems accounts for nearly all the water damage we see. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: A pipe that freezes can split, and the flooding starts once the ice thaws and pressure returns. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. Corroded fittings and worn supply lines can also let go without any warning in older homes. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: Washing machines, dishwashers and refrigerator ice makers all depend on small hoses that wear out. A water heater near the end of its life can leak from the tank bottom or fail all at once. We see many of these in Logan homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. Water that backed up out of a drain or toilet bowl is not clean, so keep kids and pets out of it.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. A sump pump that loses power or jams during a heavy rain lets the pit overflow. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. A lot of the mold we find in Logan comes from a past leak that looked dry on the surface only. A musty smell, peeling paint or dark spotting near a previous repair is worth having checked.
