Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Provo

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

This water is not clean. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Keep Out of the BasementWith the electricity still on, a flooded basement is not safe to walk into. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • The float getting caught on the side of the basin or tangled in its cord.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Low Electrical Outlets

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Have an electrician look at wet receptacles and cable before power is restored to them.

Stored Belongings

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

Furnace and Water Heater

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. A working pump should be back in the pit before our equipment leaves.
